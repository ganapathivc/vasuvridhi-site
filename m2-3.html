Settlement is when the shares and money actually change hands after a trade. India moved all equity trades to a T+1 cycle (completed in January 2023): settlement happens one working day after the trade day (T).

When you buy

DayWhat happens
T (trade day)Order executes. You must have the full amount in your trading account for delivery purchases.
T+1Clearing corporation completes settlement; shares are credited to your demat account.

When you sell

DayWhat happens
TOrder executes. Shares are blocked or debited from your demat account (authorised by DDPI or an e-DIS/TPIN confirmation).
T+1Sale proceeds are credited to your trading account, and you can withdraw them to your bank.

Who guarantees it?

The clearing corporation stands between buyer and seller. If one side fails to deliver, the clearing corporation handles it (for example through an auction), so the other side is protected.

Same-day settlement

SEBI has also introduced an optional T+0 (same-day) settlement for a limited set of shares, alongside T+1. Most investors will continue to use T+1.

Why settlement speed matters

Faster settlement reduces the time money and shares are "in transit". That lowers risk for everyone and frees your money sooner.

Key takeaways

  • India settles equity trades on T+1.
  • Buy: shares arrive next working day. Sell: money arrives next working day.
  • The clearing corporation guarantees settlement.
