Learning about markets works best in a sequence, with time to practise each idea on paper before moving on. Here is one way to use Vasuvridhi.

WeeksFocusPractice (no money needed)
1–2Module 1: Basics, and Module 2: How a trade worksWrite down, in your own words, what happens from placing an order to settlement.
3–5Module 3: Company numbersDownload one annual report of any company you know as a customer. Find revenue, profit, debt and cash flow.
6Module 5: Mutual fundsRead two fund factsheets and compare expense ratio, riskometer and benchmark.
7Module 7: RiskList your own goals and time horizons.
8Module 8: Investor protection, and Module 9: Tax basicsLook up your broker on SEBI's registered intermediaries list.
LaterModule 4: Charts, and Module 6: DerivativesUnderstand the concepts and the risks before going any further.

Habits that help

  • Keep a glossary. Write every new term and its meaning.
  • Read primary sources: annual reports, exchange filings, offer documents, SEBI investor material.
  • Be sceptical of anyone promising returns, "sure-shot" tips or urgency.
  • Go further formally if you're interested: NISM offers certification exams on securities markets and mutual funds.

What this plan is not

It is not a plan for what to invest in. Decisions about your money depend on your goals, income, obligations and risk tolerance. For personal advice, consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser.

Key takeaways

  • Learn in sequence and practise on paper.
  • Use primary sources, not tips.
  • Education first; personal advice only from SEBI-registered advisers.
