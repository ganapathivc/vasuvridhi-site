Matching investments to goals

An investment is only "good" or "bad" relative to what the money is for and when it is needed.

Start with goals, not products

Goal (example)When neededKey question
Emergency fundAny timeCan I access it quickly without loss?
Vehicle or home down-payment1–3 yearsCan I afford a fall in value just before I need it?
Child's higher education8–15 yearsHow much growth is needed, and how much volatility can I accept on the way?
Retirement20+ yearsWill it keep pace with inflation over decades?

General principles

  • Short horizon → there's little time to recover from a fall, so capital stability matters more.
  • Long horizon → there's more time to ride out volatility, and inflation becomes the bigger long-term risk.
  • Liquidity: lock-ins and exit loads matter if you might need the money.
  • Insurance first: adequate health and term-life cover, and an emergency fund, usually come before market investments.

Write it down

For each goal note the amount needed, the date, and how much uncertainty you can accept. This turns vague wishes into a plan that can be reviewed.

These are general concepts. Personal recommendations need a full view of your finances. Consult a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser for that.

Key takeaways

  • Define the goal and timeline before choosing any product.
  • Short horizons need stability; long horizons need growth and inflation protection.
  • Emergency fund and insurance come first.
