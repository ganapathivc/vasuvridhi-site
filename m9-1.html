Short-term vs long-term capital gains

When you sell an investment for more than you paid, the profit is a capital gain. How it's taxed depends mainly on the type of asset and how long you held it.

Listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds (STT paid)

Short-term (STCG)Long-term (LTCG)
Holding period12 months or lessMore than 12 months
Tax rate20%12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year
IndexationNot available

These rates have applied since 23 July 2024, and Budget 2026 left them unchanged. Surcharge and cess are added on top where applicable.

Hypothetical example

Sold within 8 monthsSold after 18 months
Gain₹80,000₹2,00,000
Exemption–₹1,25,000
Taxable gain₹80,000₹75,000
Tax (before cess)₹16,000 (20%)₹9,375 (12.5%)

Other points

  • Debt mutual funds bought on or after 1 April 2023 are generally taxed at your slab rate, regardless of holding period.
  • From 1 April 2026, buyback proceeds are taxed as capital gains for non-promoter shareholders.
  • India's new Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from 1 April 2026. Section numbers have changed from the 1961 Act, though these headline equity rates were carried forward.

Key takeaways

  • Equity held >12 months = long-term; ≤12 months = short-term.
  • STCG 20%; LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year.
  • Tax rules change. Verify the current rules each year.
