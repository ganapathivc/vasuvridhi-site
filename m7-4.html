Many investing mistakes come from how our minds work, not from a lack of information. Recognising these biases is the first defence.

BiasWhat it looks likeA counter-habit
Loss aversionHolding losing investments too long to avoid "booking" a lossAsk: "Would I buy this today at this price?"
OverconfidenceTrading too often after a few winsKeep a written record of every decision and its reasoning
Herding / FOMOBuying because "everyone is"Wait 48 hours before acting on excitement
AnchoringFixating on the price you paid or a past highFocus on fundamentals now, not old prices
Recency biasAssuming the last few months will continueLook at long histories, including bad periods
Confirmation biasSeeking only information that supports your viewDeliberately read the opposite case
Authority biasTrusting a confident influencer or a "guru"Check SEBI registration; demand evidence, not confidence

Why scammers love these biases

Fraud schemes deliberately trigger FOMO ("only today"), authority ("ex-banker with 20 years' experience") and social proof (fake screenshots of profits). Recognising the bias helps you recognise the scheme.

A simple personal rule set

  1. Write your reason before any investment decision.
  2. Never act on a tip received through messaging groups.
  3. Review decisions periodically to learn from them, not to punish yourself.

Key takeaways

  • Biases like loss aversion and FOMO drive costly mistakes.
  • Written rules and delays counter emotional decisions.
  • Scams are designed to exploit these biases.
