Leverage, the double-edged sword

Leverage means controlling a large exposure with a small amount of money. Derivatives are inherently leveraged.

Hypothetical comparison

Move in underlyingBuying shares with ₹1.8 lakhOne futures lot (₹15 lakh exposure, ₹1.8 lakh margin)
+2%+₹3,600 (+2%)+₹30,000 (+16.7%)
−2%−₹3,600 (−2%)−₹30,000 (−16.7%)
−5%−₹9,000 (−5%)−₹75,000 (−41.7%)
−12%−₹21,600 (−12%)−₹1,80,000 (−100%)

With roughly 8× leverage, a 12% adverse move wipes out the entire margin. A bigger move creates losses beyond what you deposited.

Why leverage feels attractive and is dangerous

  • Early wins feel large, which encourages bigger positions.
  • Losses compound quickly: after a 50% loss you need a 100% gain to get back to where you started.
  • Costs (STT, brokerage, slippage) apply to the full contract value, not your margin.
  • Emotional pressure leads to poor decisions (Module 7).

Key takeaways

  • Leverage magnifies gains and losses equally.
  • A small adverse move can wipe out the whole margin.
  • Losses can exceed the money deposited.
