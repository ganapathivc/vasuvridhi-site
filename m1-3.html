India has two main stock exchanges where most shares are traded.

BSENSE
Founded1875, Asia's oldest stock exchange1992 (trading began 1994)
Flagship indexS&P BSE Sensex, 30 large companiesNifty 50, 50 large companies
TradingFully electronic. Normal equity session 9:15 am – 3:30 pm IST on trading days, with a pre-open session from 9:00 am

Many companies are listed on both exchanges. The same share can trade at very slightly different prices on each at the same moment.

What an index is

An index is a single number that tracks the combined movement of a selected basket of shares. When people say "the market rose 1% today", they usually mean the Nifty 50 or the Sensex rose 1%.

How index weights work

Indian benchmark indices are free-float market-capitalisation weighted. Bigger companies carry more weight, counting only the shares actually available for trading (excluding, for example, promoter holdings).

Hypothetical 3-stock indexFree-float market capWeight
Company A₹600 crore60%
Company B₹300 crore30%
Company C₹100 crore10%

If Company A rises 5% and the other two don't move, this index rises about 3% (5% × 60%). A large company moving a little can shift the index more than a small one moving a lot.

Other indices

There are indices for mid-caps, small-caps, sectors (banking, IT, pharma), themes and more. Index funds and ETFs (Module 5) are built to copy indices like these.

Key takeaways

  • NSE and BSE are India's main stock exchanges.
  • Nifty 50 and Sensex track large companies and are free-float weighted.
  • Bigger companies influence the index more.
