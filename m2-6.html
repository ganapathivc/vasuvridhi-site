Most investor losses from fraud start with someone else gaining access to an account, or tricking the owner into acting. These habits close most of those doors.

DoDon't
Use two-factor authentication and a strong, unique passwordShare passwords, OTPs, TPINs or PINs with anyone, including "broker staff"
Install apps only via links on your broker's official website or the official app store listingInstall apps or APKs sent over WhatsApp, Telegram or SMS
Read the SMS and email alerts that exchanges and depositories send for every trade and demat debitIgnore an alert for a trade you didn't place
Check your monthly CAS from NSDL/CDSLLet anyone else operate your account "on your behalf"
Keep your mobile number and email updated with your broker and DPUse someone else's number or email on your account
Add a nomineeGive a blanket Power of Attorney to anyone other than your broker's standard DDPI

Paying an intermediary safely

Since October 2025, SEBI-registered investor-facing intermediaries such as brokers and mutual funds collect UPI payments through special "@valid" UPI IDs. You can verify a UPI ID or bank account on SEBI's SEBI Check tool before paying. Never pay a "broker" through a personal UPI ID.

If something looks wrong

  1. Change your password and log out of all devices.
  2. Write to your broker and DP immediately and keep the acknowledgement.
  3. Report cyber fraud at cybercrime.gov.in or call the 1930 helpline.
  4. If the broker doesn't resolve it, escalate through SEBI's SCORES portal (Module 8).

Key takeaways

  • Never share OTPs, TPINs or passwords.
  • Read every trade and demat alert.
  • Pay intermediaries only through verified '@valid' UPI IDs or verified bank accounts.
