How a mutual fund works

A mutual fund pools money from many investors and invests it according to a stated objective, such as large-company shares, government bonds or a mix. Each investor owns units in proportion to the money they put in.

Who's involved

EntityRole
SponsorSets up the fund (like a promoter)
TrusteesOversee the fund in the interest of unit holders
Asset Management Company (AMC)Manages the money: the fund managers and research team
CustodianHolds the fund's securities safely
RTAMaintains investor records and processes transactions
SEBIRegulates the whole structure

A mutual fund is set up as a trust. The investments belong to the unit holders, not to the AMC.

Open-ended vs closed-ended

Open-endedClosed-ended
Buy/sell with the fundAny business dayOnly at launch; redemption at maturity
LiquidityHigh (subject to exit loads)Listed on exchange, but trading may be thin

Benefits and trade-offs

  • Diversification with small amounts.
  • Professional management, but for a fee (expense ratio).
  • No guaranteed returns. The value of units rises and falls with the underlying investments.

Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks; read all scheme-related documents carefully.

Key takeaways

  • A mutual fund is a trust that pools money and issues units.
  • The AMC manages; trustees and SEBI oversee.
  • Returns are never guaranteed.
