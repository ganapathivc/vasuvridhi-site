SEBI has published a series of studies on how individual traders fare in the equity derivatives (F&O) segment. The findings have been consistent.

Study periodKey finding (as reported)
FY22 – FY24About 93% of individual F&O traders incurred losses over the three years.
FY25About 91% of individual traders made net losses. Aggregate net losses rose to over ₹1 lakh crore.
FY26About 87.7% of individual traders incurred net losses. Aggregate net losses were about ₹91,685 crore.

Other patterns reported

  • Transaction costs alone turned many small gains into net losses.
  • Losses were persistent: traders who lost in earlier years were highly likely to lose again.
  • Very few traders were consistently profitable over multiple years.

What SEBI did

From November 2024, SEBI raised minimum contract sizes for index derivatives, limited weekly expiries to one benchmark index per exchange, required upfront collection of option premiums and added margin on expiry days. The government also raised STT on futures and options from 1 April 2026.

What this means for a learner

Understand derivatives so you can recognise the risks and the marketing around them. Treat claims of steady income from F&O trading with deep scepticism.

Source: SEBI press releases and studies on profitability of individual traders in the equity derivatives segment (sebi.gov.in). Figures as reported at publication; see SEBI's website for the full studies.

Key takeaways

  • Roughly 9 in 10 individual F&O traders lost money in SEBI's studies.
  • Costs and persistence of losses are major factors.
  • SEBI has tightened F&O rules because of these findings.
