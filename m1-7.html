Corporate actions are decisions by a company that change what shareholders hold or receive. Each is announced to the exchanges, with a record date: you must be a shareholder on record that day to be eligible. Under T+1 settlement, the ex-date (the first day a buyer no longer gets the benefit) is usually the same as the record date.

ActionWhat happensEffect on your holding (hypothetical)
DividendCompany pays cash per share from profits₹5 dividend on 100 shares = ₹500 credited to bank. Share price typically drops by about the dividend on the ex-date.
Bonus issueFree extra shares from reserves, e.g. 1:1100 shares at ₹400 become 200 shares at about ₹200. Total value is unchanged.
Stock splitFace value is divided, e.g. ₹10 into ₹2 (1:5)100 shares at ₹1,000 become 500 shares at about ₹200. Total value is unchanged.
BuybackCompany buys its own shares back, often at a premiumYou may tender shares. Fewer shares remain outstanding.
Rights issueRight to buy new shares, usually at a discountYou can subscribe, sell the entitlement, or let it lapse.

Common misunderstanding

Bonus issues and splits do not create value by themselves. The pie is cut into more slices, and the size of the pie doesn't change. They may make the share price look "affordable", but your ownership percentage stays the same.

Tax note

Dividends are taxed at your income-tax slab rate. From 1 April 2026, money received in a buyback is taxed as capital gains for ordinary (non-promoter) shareholders. See Module 9 for the basics.

Key takeaways

  • Check the record date to know who is eligible.
  • Bonus and splits change the number of shares, not the value.
  • Dividends reduce the share price by roughly the dividend amount.
