What is a share and why does it have a price?

A share is a small unit of ownership in a company. If a company has divided itself into 1 crore shares and you own 1,000 of them, you own 0.01% of that business: a tiny slice of its assets, its profits and its future.

What owning a share gives you

RightWhat it means
Share in profitsThe company may pay part of its profit to shareholders as a dividend. It isn't obliged to.
VotingShareholders vote on important matters such as appointing directors, at the AGM or through e-voting.
InformationListed companies must publish results, annual reports and material news.
Limited liabilityIf the company fails, the most you can lose is what you paid for the shares.

Face value vs market price

Face value is the nominal value printed on the share (often ₹1, ₹2, ₹5 or ₹10). It is an accounting figure. The market price is what buyers and sellers agree on at any moment on the exchange, and it can be far above or below face value.

Why does the price move?

A share price is set by demand and supply. When more people want to buy than sell at the current price, the price rises until enough sellers appear, and the reverse is also true. Behind that demand are people's expectations about the company's future profits, interest rates, the economy, news, and plain sentiment.

Hypothetical example

Company A (fictional)Value
Total shares1,00,00,000
Market price per share₹250
Market capitalisation (shares × price)₹250 crore

Market capitalisation is what the market as a whole is currently valuing the company's equity at. It is not the same as what the business is "really" worth. That question is covered in Module 3.

Key takeaways

  • A share is part-ownership of a business.
  • Price is set by buyers and sellers, and reflects expectations, not certainty.
  • Market cap = number of shares × share price.
