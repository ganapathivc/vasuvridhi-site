A futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell the underlying at a fixed price on a future date. Both sides are obliged to complete it.

Hypothetical example

ItemValue
UnderlyingFictional index at 20,000
Lot size75 units
Contract value20,000 × 75 = ₹15,00,000
Margin required (illustrative ~12%)₹1,80,000

Mark-to-market (MTM)

Every trading day, futures positions are revalued at that day's settlement price, and gains or losses are settled in cash.

DaySettlement priceDaily MTM (bought 1 lot)Running P&L
120,100+₹7,500+₹7,500
219,800−₹22,500−₹15,000
319,500−₹22,500−₹37,500

A 2.5% fall in the underlying (20,000 to 19,500) caused a loss of ₹37,500, about 21% of the ₹1.8 lakh margin. If the margin falls below the requirement, you must add money or the position may be closed.

Why futures are risky for individuals

  • Losses are not limited to the margin; they can exceed it.
  • Daily MTM needs cash on hand.
  • Leverage magnifies small price moves.

Key takeaways

  • Futures oblige both sides to complete the contract.
  • Gains and losses are settled daily (MTM).
  • Losses can exceed the margin deposited.
