To invest in shares you need two accounts, usually opened together with one broker.

Trading accountDemat account
PurposePlace buy and sell ordersHold shares electronically
AnalogyThe shop counterThe locker
Held withYour stock brokerA depository (NSDL/CDSL) through a DP

A linked bank account completes the "3-in-1" set-up: money goes from the bank to buy, and sale proceeds come back to the bank.

Opening an account: what's involved

  • KYC: PAN, Aadhaar or other address proof, a photo, bank details, and verification (often online through video or Aadhaar OTP).
  • Nomination: name who should receive your holdings if something happens to you, or formally opt out.
  • Risk disclosures: you'll be shown documents explaining market risks. Read them.
  • Segment activation: cash/equity is standard. Derivatives need extra proof of income and carry high risk (Module 6).

Charges to look for

ChargeWhat it is
Account openingOne-time; some brokers charge nothing
Annual maintenance (AMC)Yearly fee for the demat account. Small holders may qualify for a lower-cost Basic Services Demat Account (BSDA).
DP chargeA fee each time shares are debited from your demat account on selling
BrokerageFee per order; varies by broker and segment

Your shares are yours

Shares in your demat account are held in your name at the depository. Each month you should receive a Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) from NSDL/CDSL showing all holdings. Checking it is one of the simplest ways to catch anything unusual.

Key takeaways

  • Trading account = place orders; demat account = hold shares.
  • Complete nomination when opening the account.
  • Check your monthly CAS from NSDL/CDSL.
