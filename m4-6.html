Why no chart pattern is a guarantee

Chart patterns and indicators are widely discussed, but it's important to understand their limits before trusting any of them.

LimitationWhy it matters
HindsightPatterns look obvious on old charts because you already know what happened next.
SubjectivityTwo people looking at the same chart often see different patterns.
RandomnessRandom price series also produce shapes that look like patterns.
SurvivorshipExamples shown online tend to be the ones that "worked"; failures are rarely posted.
Over-fittingA rule tuned to fit past data perfectly often fails on new data.
CostsFrequent trading adds brokerage, STT and other charges (Module 2), which reduce any edge.
NewsResults, regulation or events can override any chart in a moment.

What SEBI's data shows

SEBI's studies of individual traders have consistently found that most active derivative traders lose money (Module 6). Short-term trading based on charts is a difficult, competitive activity, not a reliable source of income.

A healthy way to treat this module

Understanding charts helps you read what others are talking about and recognise exaggerated claims. It is not a shortcut to profits, and no course, indicator or "system" can guarantee results.

Key takeaways

  • Patterns are clearer in hindsight than in real time.
  • Costs and randomness erode most short-term 'edges'.
  • Be wary of anyone claiming a chart method guarantees profit.
