To make funds easier to compare, SEBI requires each scheme to fit a defined category with rules on what it can invest in. Each fund house can generally offer only one scheme per category (with some exceptions such as index funds and ETFs).

Market-cap definitions

For equity categories, SEBI defines large caps as the top 100 listed companies by market capitalisation, mid caps as companies 101–250, and small caps as 251 onwards. AMFI publishes the updated list periodically.

Why categories matter

When comparing funds, compare within the same category and against the category's benchmark. Comparing a small-cap fund with a liquid fund is meaningless.

Key takeaways

  • Every scheme belongs to a SEBI-defined category.
  • Large cap = top 100, mid cap = 101–250, small cap = 251 onwards.
  • Compare funds only within the same category.
