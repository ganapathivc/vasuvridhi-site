An option gives the buyer a right, but not an obligation. The seller (writer) takes on the obligation in exchange for receiving a premium.

Call optionPut option
Buyer's rightTo buy at the strike priceTo sell at the strike price
Buyer gains ifPrice rises well above strike + premiumPrice falls well below strike − premium
Buyer's maximum lossPremium paidPremium paid
Seller's maximum lossUnlimited in theoryLarge (down to zero price)

Hypothetical call option at expiry

Fictional index at 20,000. Buy a 20,200 call for a premium of ₹100 per unit, lot of 75 (premium paid ₹7,500 upfront).

Index at expiryValue of call per unitBuyer's net per unitBuyer's net for lot
19,8000−100−₹7,500
20,2000−100−₹7,500
20,3001000₹0 (breakeven)
20,500300+200+₹15,000

The index must rise beyond the breakeven point (strike + premium) before the buyer makes anything, and it has to happen before expiry.

Time decay

An option's premium includes "time value", which shrinks as expiry approaches. All else equal, an option buyer loses a little value each day.

Rules worth knowing

  • Option buyers must pay the full premium upfront.
  • Option sellers must keep substantial margins.
  • From 1 April 2026, STT on the sale of options is 0.15% of the premium.

Key takeaways

  • Calls = right to buy; puts = right to sell.
  • Buyers risk the premium; sellers can face very large losses.
  • Options lose time value as expiry approaches.
