NAV: Net Asset Value

NAV per unit = (Value of all investments + cash − liabilities) ÷ Number of units

Hypothetical fundAmount
Market value of holdings₹505 crore
Cash₹5 crore
Liabilities (incl. accrued expenses)₹10 crore
Units outstanding10 crore
NAV₹50.00

NAV is calculated at the end of each business day. A "low" NAV doesn't make a fund cheap. A ₹10 NAV and a ₹500 NAV fund are not comparable on that basis; what matters is how the underlying investments perform.

Expense ratio (TER)

The Total Expense Ratio is the annual cost of running the fund, deducted from the fund's assets daily, so it's already reflected in the NAV. SEBI caps TER based on fund type and size. A 1% difference in TER every year compounds into a large difference over decades.

Exit load

An exit load is a charge on redeeming within a specified period, for example 1% if redeemed within 12 months (illustrative). It discourages short-term in-and-out movements. Check the scheme's documents for its actual exit load.

Where to find these

Scheme Information Document (SID), Key Information Memorandum (KIM), monthly factsheets, and the AMC and AMFI websites.

Key takeaways

  • NAV = net value of the fund's assets per unit.
  • TER is deducted daily and lowers your return.
  • Check exit loads before investing.
