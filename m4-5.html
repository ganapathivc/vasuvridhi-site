A moving average smooths price data by averaging the last few periods, updating as each new period arrives.

Simple moving average (SMA): hypothetical example

SMA vs EMA

SMAEMA (exponential)
WeightingAll periods equalMore weight on recent periods
Reaction to new pricesSlowerFaster

How people use them, and the limits

  • A longer average (for example 200-day) is used to describe the long-term direction; a shorter one (for example 20-day) the recent direction.
  • Moving averages lag, because they are built from past prices, so they confirm a move only after it has happened.
  • In sideways markets, crossovers of two averages often give repeated false signals.

Key takeaways

  • A moving average is the average of recent prices, updated each period.
  • EMA reacts faster than SMA.
  • All moving averages lag the price.
