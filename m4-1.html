A price chart plots price on the vertical axis against time on the horizontal axis. The three common styles show the same data in different detail.

ChartShowsGood for
LineUsually the closing price of each period, joined by a lineSeeing the broad direction at a glance
Bar (OHLC)Open, High, Low, Close of each period as a vertical bar with ticksSeeing the range of each period
CandlestickThe same OHLC data, with a filled "body" between open and closeSeeing quickly whether each period closed up or down

Anatomy of a candlestick

  • The body spans the open and close prices.
  • The thin lines, called wicks or shadows, show the high and low of the period.
  • Each candle can represent any period: a minute, a day, a week.

A reminder

A chart is a record of what already happened. It describes past trading. It doesn't contain information about the company's future earnings, and it doesn't tell you what will happen next.

Key takeaways

  • Line, bar and candlestick charts show the same prices in different detail.
  • A candle shows open, high, low and close for one period.
  • Charts describe the past; they don't predict the future.
