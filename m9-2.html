How dividends are taxed

Dividends received from Indian companies and mutual funds are added to your income and taxed at your income-tax slab rate.

PointRule (resident individuals)
Who pays the taxThe shareholder (the earlier Dividend Distribution Tax paid by companies was abolished from 2020-21)
RateYour applicable slab rate
TDS10% deducted by the company/fund if dividends from it exceed ₹10,000 in a financial year (20% if PAN isn't available)
Nil-TDS declarationEligible individuals with income below the taxable limit can submit the prescribed declaration (traditionally Forms 15G/15H)

Hypothetical example

You receive ₹15,000 in dividends from Company A during the year. Since this exceeds ₹10,000, the company deducts ₹1,500 TDS and pays you ₹13,500. You report the full ₹15,000 as income, compute tax at your slab rate, and claim credit for the ₹1,500 already deducted.

Check your records

TDS on dividends appears in your Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS) on the income-tax portal. Match these with the dividends credited to your bank.

Key takeaways

  • Dividends are taxed at your slab rate.
  • TDS of 10% applies above ₹10,000 per payer per year.
  • Match dividends with AIS/26AS when filing.
