An annual report is the most complete document a listed company publishes each year. It's long, but you don't need to read every page.

Where to find it

On the company's website (Investor Relations section) and in the company's filings on the NSE and BSE websites.

A practical reading order

#SectionLook for
1Chairman's / MD's letterManagement's view of the year. Compare it with what was promised in earlier years.
2Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A)Industry conditions, segment performance, risks, outlook
3Financial statements (consolidated)P&L, balance sheet, cash flow (Chapters 1–3)
4Notes to accountsAccounting policies, borrowings, contingent liabilities, related-party transactions
5Independent auditor's reportIs the opinion "unmodified"? Any qualifications, emphasis of matter or key audit matters?
6Corporate governance reportBoard composition, independent directors, attendance, remuneration
7Shareholding patternPromoter holding and pledged shares
8BRSRBusiness Responsibility and Sustainability Report: environmental and social disclosures

Questions worth asking

  • Has the auditor changed recently, and why?
  • Are related-party transactions large relative to profits?
  • Are contingent liabilities (for example pending lawsuits or guarantees) large compared to net worth?
  • Have promoters pledged a significant share of their holding?

Key takeaways

  • Start with MD&A, financials, notes and the auditor's report.
  • Notes often contain the most important details.
  • Auditor qualifications and large related-party deals deserve attention.
