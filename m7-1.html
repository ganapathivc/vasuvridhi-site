In finance, risk usually means uncertainty: the chance that the outcome differs from what you expected, including the chance of loss. Investments that offer the possibility of higher returns almost always carry higher risk.

A broad, qualitative comparison

Asset typeTypical risk levelMain risks
Bank savings / fixed depositsLowInflation eroding real value
Government bondsLow to moderateInterest-rate changes affect prices
Corporate bonds / debt fundsModerateCredit (default) risk, interest-rate risk
Large-company sharesHighMarket falls, business risk
Small-company sharesVery highSharp volatility, liquidity, business failure
Derivatives (as speculation)Very highLeverage, losses beyond capital

Volatility and drawdown

  • Volatility is how much prices swing around their average.
  • Drawdown is the fall from a peak to a trough.

The maths of losses

LossGain needed to recover
−10%+11%
−25%+33%
−50%+100%
−75%+300%

This is why avoiding large losses matters so much. Any product promising high returns with "no risk" deserves deep suspicion.

Key takeaways

  • Higher potential return comes with higher risk.
  • Large losses need much larger gains to recover.
  • 'High return, zero risk' is a red flag.
