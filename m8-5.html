SEBI requires market intermediaries such as brokers, depository participants, mutual funds, Investment Advisers and Research Analysts to publish an Investor Charter. It sets out what investors can expect and what they should do.

Typical rights (summarised)

You can expect
Fair and equal treatment
Clear information about services, fees and charges before you commit
Timely execution, settlement and statements
Confidentiality of your personal information
A grievance redressal mechanism and access to SCORES / ODR
Advice or recommendations (from registered IAs/RAs) based on documented reasoning, with conflicts disclosed

Typical responsibilities (summarised)

You should
Deal only with SEBI-registered intermediaries
Give accurate KYC information and keep contact details updated
Read documents before signing, and keep copies
Check statements, contract notes and alerts regularly
Never share passwords, OTPs or PINs
Not trust promises of assured returns

Read the actual charter on the website of each intermediary you deal with, as each category's charter differs in detail.

Key takeaways

  • Every intermediary category has a SEBI-mandated Investor Charter.
  • It lists your rights and your responsibilities.
  • Read the charter of each intermediary you use.
