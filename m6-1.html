A derivative is a contract whose value is derived from something else, called the underlying, such as a share, an index, a currency or a commodity.

Common types

TypeBasic idea
ForwardPrivate agreement to buy/sell later at a fixed price (not exchange-traded)
FuturesStandardised, exchange-traded version of a forward
OptionsThe right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell at a fixed price
SwapsExchanging cash-flow streams (mostly used by institutions)

Why derivatives exist

  • Hedging: reducing risk. For example, a farmer locking in a selling price, or an importer fixing an exchange rate.
  • Price discovery: derivative markets reflect expectations.
  • Speculation: taking a view on price movement with leverage. This is where most individual losses happen.

Key features of exchange-traded equity derivatives in India

  • Traded in fixed lot sizes; index derivative contracts must have a minimum value of ₹15 lakh at introduction (since November 2024).
  • Have an expiry date. Each exchange may offer weekly expiry on only one benchmark index.
  • Require margin and are settled through the clearing corporation.
High-risk segment. SEBI's studies show that most individual traders in equity derivatives lose money. See the last chapter of this module.

Key takeaways

  • A derivative's value depends on an underlying asset.
  • Designed for hedging, often used for speculation.
  • Exchange-traded derivatives have lots, expiries and margins.
