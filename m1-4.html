Buying a single share involves several institutions working together. Knowing who does what helps you understand where your money and shares sit, and whom to approach if something goes wrong.

InstitutionRoleExamples
SEBIThe regulator. Makes rules, registers intermediaries, protects investors and acts against wrongdoing.Securities and Exchange Board of India
Stock exchangesRun the marketplace where buy and sell orders are matched.NSE, BSE
Clearing corporationsGuarantee that trades settle: buyers get shares and sellers get money, even if a counterparty defaults.NSE Clearing, Indian Clearing Corporation
DepositoriesHold your shares electronically, like a bank holds money.NSDL, CDSL
Depository Participants (DPs)Open and service your demat account on behalf of a depository.Most brokers and many banks
Stock brokersMembers of exchanges who place your orders.SEBI-registered brokers
RTAsRegistrars and transfer agents keep shareholder records for companies and process IPO allotments.Registrars named in offer documents
Investment Advisers / Research AnalystsThe only people legally allowed to give investment advice or recommendations for a fee, after registering with SEBI.Check SEBI's registered list

Following one trade

  1. You place an order through your broker.
  2. The exchange matches it with a seller.
  3. The clearing corporation settles the trade.
  4. The depository moves the shares into your demat account.

All of these are regulated by SEBI. Before dealing with anyone, you can check their registration (see Module 8).

Key takeaways

  • SEBI regulates; exchanges match; clearing corporations guarantee; depositories hold.
  • Your shares sit with NSDL or CDSL, not with your broker.
  • Only SEBI-registered IAs/RAs may give investment advice.
