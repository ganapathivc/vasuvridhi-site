What the P/E ratio tells you, and what it doesn't

Published 18 November 2026 · Education only

The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio is one of the first numbers new investors meet. It is useful, but it is easy to misread.

The formula

P/E = Share price ÷ Earnings per share (EPS)

A hypothetical example

Imagine Company A (fictional). Its share price is ₹200 and its EPS over the last year was ₹10.

ItemCompany A (hypothetical)
Share price₹200
EPS (trailing 12 months)₹10
P/E20

A P/E of 20 means investors are paying ₹20 for every ₹1 of the company's last-year earnings.

What P/E does not tell you

  • Quality of earnings: one-time gains can inflate EPS.
  • Debt: two firms with the same P/E can carry very different debt.
  • Growth: a high P/E may reflect growth expectations, which may or may not happen.
  • Industry context: P/E levels vary widely across sectors, so compare like with like.

A "low" P/E is not automatically cheap, and a "high" P/E is not automatically expensive. Treat it as a starting question, not an answer.
