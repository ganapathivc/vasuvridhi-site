Margin is money (or pledged securities) you must keep with your broker to cover the risk of a position. Exchanges and clearing corporations set minimum margins so that losses can be paid even if prices move sharply.

Margin in different situations

SituationHow margin works
Delivery purchase (cash)You pay the full value. Collecting margin upfront is mandatory.
Intraday equityBrokers may allow positions with less than full value, but SEBI's peak-margin rules cap the leverage they can give.
Futures & options sellingMargin is set by the exchange's risk model (SPAN plus exposure margin) and can be a large amount.
Margin Trading Facility (MTF)Broker lends part of the purchase amount. You pay interest and must top up if prices fall.
PledgeYou pledge shares you own to get margin for other trades; a haircut is applied to their value.

Why leverage is dangerous

Margin lets you control a position bigger than your money, and that magnifies both gains and losses.

HypotheticalWithout leverageWith 4× leverage
Your money₹50,000₹50,000
Position size₹50,000₹2,00,000
Price falls 10%Loss ₹5,000 (−10%)Loss ₹20,000 (−40%)

Margin calls

If losses reduce your margin below the required level, the broker will ask you to add funds. If you don't, it can square off your position at whatever the price is then.

Key takeaways

  • Margin protects the system against losses.
  • Leverage magnifies losses as much as gains.
  • Borrowed positions (MTF) cost interest and can be closed out on you.
