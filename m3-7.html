Intrinsic value is an estimate of what a business is worth based on the cash it can generate in the future, independent of its current market price. It is always an estimate, built on assumptions.

The idea behind discounted cash flow (DCF)

₹100 received five years from now is worth less than ₹100 today, because today's money could earn a return meanwhile. A DCF estimates future free cash flows and discounts them back to today's value.

Simplified hypothetical example

AssumptionValue
Free cash flow next year₹60 crore
Growth rate (assumed constant, forever)5%
Discount rate (required return)12%
Value = FCF ÷ (discount rate − growth)60 ÷ (0.12 − 0.05) ≈ ₹857 crore

How sensitive is it?

Growth assumedDiscount rateEstimated value
4%12%₹750 cr
5%12%₹857 cr
6%12%₹1,000 cr
5%10%₹1,200 cr

Small changes in assumptions produce large changes in value. This is why two careful analysts can reach very different conclusions.

Margin of safety

Because estimates can be wrong, value investors look for a gap between their estimated value and the price paid: a margin of safety. It is a cushion against error, not a guarantee.

This chapter explains a concept. It does not value any real company or suggest any security is under- or over-priced.

Key takeaways

  • Intrinsic value is an estimate of future cash, discounted to today.
  • Results are very sensitive to assumptions.
  • A margin of safety is a cushion against your own errors.
