After every trading day with transactions, your broker sends a contract note: the legal record of your trades and every charge applied. Learn to read it, because costs reduce returns.

Charges on an equity delivery trade

ChargeWho sets itRate (equity delivery)
BrokerageYour brokerVaries; some charge zero for delivery
Securities Transaction Tax (STT)Government0.1% on buy and 0.1% on sell
Exchange transaction chargesExchangeA small percentage of turnover
SEBI turnover feeSEBI₹10 per crore of turnover
Stamp dutyGovernment0.015% on the buy side
GSTGovernment18% on brokerage, exchange and SEBI charges
DP chargeDepository/DPFlat fee per scrip when shares leave your demat on selling

For intraday equity, STT is 0.025% on the sell side only and stamp duty is 0.003% on the buy side. Derivatives have their own rates. From 1 April 2026, STT on futures is 0.05% and on options 0.15% of premium on the sell side.

Hypothetical example: buy ₹1,00,000 of shares (delivery)

ItemApprox. amount
STT (0.1%)₹100
Stamp duty (0.015%)₹15
Exchange + SEBI charges (illustrative)~₹4
GST on those charges (if brokerage is zero)~₹1
Total cost to buy~₹120

Selling attracts STT again, plus the DP charge. Frequent trading multiplies these costs.

What to check on every contract note

  • Trade price, quantity and time match what you placed.
  • No trades you didn't place.
  • Charges look consistent with your broker's published tariff.

Report any discrepancy to your broker immediately, in writing.

Key takeaways

  • Every trade has taxes and fees beyond brokerage.
  • STT on delivery is 0.1% on both buy and sell.
  • Check each contract note for unauthorised trades.
