Both index funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) are usually passive: they aim to copy an index rather than beat it.

Index fundETF
How you buy/sellDirectly with the AMC or via a platform, at the day's NAVOn the stock exchange through a broker, at live market prices
Accounts neededNo demat neededDemat and trading account
SIPEasily availablePossible with some brokers
CostsExpense ratioExpense ratio + brokerage + bid-ask spread
Liquidity riskAMC redeems unitsDepends on trading volume; may trade away from NAV

Tracking error and tracking difference

  • Tracking difference: how much the fund's return differed from the index's over a period, mostly due to costs.
  • Tracking error: how much that difference fluctuates. Lower is better; it's disclosed in factsheets.

Things to understand

  • A passive fund will fall when the index falls. It removes fund-manager risk, not market risk.
  • For an ETF, check its trading volume and the gap between its market price and its indicative NAV (iNAV).

Key takeaways

  • Index funds and ETFs aim to match an index, not beat it.
  • ETFs trade on exchanges and need a demat account.
  • Check tracking error, costs and (for ETFs) liquidity.
