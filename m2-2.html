The order book is the exchange's live list of all pending buy and sell limit orders for a security. Many trading apps show the top five levels as "market depth".

A hypothetical order book

Bid qtyBid price (buyers)Ask price (sellers)Ask qty
300₹99.90₹100.10150
500₹99.85₹100.15400
1,200₹99.80₹100.20900
  • Best bid (₹99.90): the highest price someone is willing to pay.
  • Best ask (₹100.10): the lowest price someone is willing to sell at.
  • Spread: the gap (₹0.20). A narrow spread usually signals a liquid security.

How orders are matched

Exchanges use price-time priority: the best price is matched first, and among orders at the same price, the earliest is matched first. A trade happens when a buyer's price meets a seller's price.

Liquidity

A liquid share has many orders on both sides and a tight spread, so you can buy or sell without moving the price much. An illiquid share may have wide spreads and thin quantities, so even small orders can move the price, and exiting can be hard.

Price bands and circuits

Exchanges set price bands on many shares, limiting how far the price can move in a day, and index-wide circuit breakers that halt trading after very large market moves. When a share hits its band, the order book may show only buyers or only sellers.

Key takeaways

  • Bid = buyers, ask = sellers, spread = the gap.
  • Orders match by price, then time.
  • Thin order books mean higher costs and harder exits.
