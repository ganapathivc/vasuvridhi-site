Market orders and limit orders

When you place an order you choose how it should be executed. The two basic types are market orders and limit orders.

Market orderLimit order
Instruction"Buy/sell now at the best available price""Buy/sell only at this price or better"
ExecutionAlmost certain (if there are counterparties)Not guaranteed; may stay pending
PriceNot guaranteedGuaranteed limit
RiskCan fill at an unexpected price in a thin or fast marketMay miss the trade entirely

Hypothetical example

A fictional share last traded at ₹100. The best sellers are offering 50 shares at ₹100.10 and 200 at ₹100.50.

  • A market buy for 150 shares fills 50 at ₹100.10 and 100 at ₹100.50, an average of about ₹100.37.
  • A limit buy at ₹100.10 for 150 shares fills 50 immediately. The remaining 100 wait until someone sells at ₹100.10 or lower.

Other order types you'll see

TypeMeaning
Stop-loss (SL)Becomes a limit order once a trigger price is hit
Stop-loss market (SL-M)Becomes a market order once triggered (not available in all segments)
After-market order (AMO)Placed outside market hours, sent when the market opens
Validity: Day / IOCDay orders expire at close; Immediate-or-Cancel fills what it can at once and cancels the rest

A stop-loss order is a tool to limit losses, but it doesn't guarantee the exit price. In a sharp fall, prices can jump past the trigger.

Key takeaways

  • Market orders prioritise execution; limit orders prioritise price.
  • In illiquid shares, market orders can fill at poor prices.
  • Stop-loss orders limit, but don't guarantee, exit prices.
